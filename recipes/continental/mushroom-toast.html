Crispy Oyster Mushroom Toast

Café-style Brunch

Time
15 min
Difficulty
Easy
Serves
2
Cuisine
Continental

Sourdough grilled in olive oil, spread with labneh, piled with roasted pink oyster mushrooms crisped at their frilled edges. Chilli flakes, herb oil, a squeeze of lemon. The dish dominating café menus from Melbourne to Mumbai.

Ingredients
  • 200 g Pink Oyster mushrooms, kept in frond clusters
  • 2 thick slices sourdough bread
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 3 cloves garlic — 2 sliced, 1 whole (for rubbing toast)
  • 4 tbsp labneh or thick Greek yoghurt
  • 1/2 tsp chilli flakes
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • Flaky salt and black pepper
  • Fresh herbs parsley, chives, or dill — a small handful
  • 1 tsp cold-pressed coconut oil or herb-infused oil (for finish)
Method
  1. 01Season labneh with a pinch of salt and a few drops of lemon juice. Set aside.
  2. 02Grill or toast the sourdough slices on both sides in a dry cast-iron pan until the surface is charred at the edges. While still hot, rub one side firmly with the whole garlic clove. The rough bread acts as a grater. Drizzle with olive oil.
  3. 03In the same pan, heat 2 tbsp olive oil and 2 tbsp butter over high heat. Add sliced garlic and let it sizzle for 20 seconds. Add the mushroom clusters in a single layer — frond-side down if possible. Do not move for 2 minutes.
  4. 04Toss, season with salt and pepper, and cook for another 2 minutes. The frills should be deeply golden and crisped at their tips. Remove from heat and squeeze a little lemon juice over the top.
  5. 05Spread the labneh generously on the toasted bread. Pile the mushrooms on top — layered, not flat. Scatter chilli flakes, fresh herbs, and a final drizzle of coconut or herb oil. Finish with flaky salt.
  6. 06Eat immediately. The contrast of the crisp toast, cool labneh, and hot mushroom is the whole point.
Cook's Note
Pink oyster mushrooms are the most dramatic choice here — the frilled edges crisp like lace and their coral deepens to a gorgeous burnt-orange against the white labneh. The cold-pressed coconut oil in the finish is our touch — it bridges the South Indian pantry and the café plate. If you have curry-leaf-infused oil, even better.
