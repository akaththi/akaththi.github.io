Ingredients
  • 2 clusters Elm or Grey Oyster mushrooms (large, kept whole — about 200 g each)
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 4 cloves garlic, smashed
  • 4 sprigs fresh thyme
  • Flaky salt and black pepper
  • SAUCE
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 1 shallot finely minced
  • 1 tsp cracked black pepper (generous)
  • 50 ml cognac or brandy (optional)
  • 100 ml fresh cream
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • Salt to taste
Method
  1. 01Pat the mushroom clusters completely dry with a cloth. Season both sides generously with salt and pepper. Place a second heavy pan, cast iron skillet, or a foil-wrapped brick on top of the cluster to act as a press.
  2. 02Heat a cast-iron skillet on high heat until it begins to smoke lightly. Add olive oil, then immediately place the mushroom clusters in, pressed-side down. Set the weight on top.
  3. 03Press and sear for 3–4 minutes without moving until the bottom is deeply dark and crackling. Flip, add butter, garlic, and thyme to the pan. Baste continuously by tilting the pan and spooning the foaming butter over the mushroom for 2–3 minutes.
  4. 04Remove mushrooms to a warm plate. Rest for 5 minutes.
  5. 05In the same pan, reduce heat to medium. Add 1 tbsp butter and shallot — cook 2 minutes. Add cracked pepper, stir 30 seconds. If using cognac, add and let it bubble until almost gone. Add cream and mustard, stir until the sauce thickens to coat a spoon. Taste and adjust salt.
  6. 06Plate the mushroom steak over roasted potatoes or wilted greens. Spoon sauce around it. Finish with flaky salt.
Cook's Note
The pressing technique is borrowed from the pressed duck method — it creates maximum surface contact with the hot pan and drives out moisture rapidly, giving a crust that meat-eaters have trouble believing is mushroom. Elm oyster clusters are structurally rigid enough to hold together through this treatment. Keep the cluster intact — do not separate the fronds. The whole cluster is the steak.
