Golden Oyster / Pink Oyster

Creamy Oyster Mushroom Pasta

Continental

Time
20 min
Difficulty
Easy
Serves
2
Cuisine
Continental

Torn oyster clusters sautéed until golden, finished in a cream and garlic sauce that coats pasta with the weight you'd normally only get from a long-cooked ragu. Twenty minutes. Restaurant depth.

Ingredients
  • 250 g Golden or Pink Oyster mushrooms, torn
  • 250 g pasta — tagliatelle, pappardelle, or linguine
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 5 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 150 ml heavy cream
  • 60 g Parmesan, finely grated (plus more for serving)
  • 1/2 cup pasta cooking water (reserve before draining)
  • 1 tsp lemon zest
  • Fresh parsley chopped, for serving
  • Salt & black pepper generously
Method
  1. 01Cook pasta in heavily salted, boiling water until al dente. Reserve 1/2 cup of pasta water before draining. Drain and set aside.
  2. 02In a wide pan, heat butter and olive oil over high heat. Add mushrooms in a single layer — do not stir. Let them sear for 2 minutes until golden at the base. Toss and repeat on the other side. Season generously with salt and pepper.
  3. 03Reduce heat to medium. Add garlic and cook for 90 seconds until fragrant and lightly golden at the edges.
  4. 04Add cream and bring to a simmer. Let reduce for 2 minutes. Add 3–4 tablespoons of the reserved pasta water to loosen the sauce to a silky consistency.
  5. 05Add the drained pasta directly to the sauce. Toss to coat, adding more pasta water if needed. Remove from heat and add Parmesan, tossing as it melts into the sauce.
  6. 06Finish with lemon zest, fresh parsley, and a few turns of black pepper. Serve immediately in warm bowls.
Cook's Note
Golden oyster turns this pasta visually spectacular — the mushrooms go from their natural pale gold to deep amber in the pan, and the cream picks up those golden tones. Pink oyster is even more dramatic, tinting the cream a gentle rose. Both are worth trying. The pasta water is not optional — it contains starch that binds the sauce to the pasta in a way cream alone cannot.
