Rahul Sharma — Nungambakkam, Chennai
I moved from Tokyo five years ago. For the longest time I could not find oyster mushrooms here that actually tasted like oyster mushrooms. Akaththi grey oyster changed that. My Sunday ramen ritual is back.

Rahul's ramen is a Sunday ritual — built slowly, eaten quietly. He sources his miso and kombu from a small Japanese grocery in Nungambakkam and credits grey oyster mushroom for being the only local ingredient that genuinely replaces what he had in Tokyo.

Ingredients
  • 200 g Grey Oyster mushrooms, kept as full clusters
  • 2 portions fresh or dried ramen noodles
  • 900 ml dashi stock (kombu + bonito, or kombu-only for vegetarian)
  • 2.5 tbsp white miso paste
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp mirin
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 2 soft-boiled eggs, marinated in soy and mirin (ajitsuke tamago)
  • 2 sheets nori, halved
  • 2 spring onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tsp white sesame seeds
  • Chilli oil or togarashi for heat
Rahul's Method
  1. 01The eggs take longest — prepare them first or the night before. Boil 6 minutes 30 seconds, ice bath, peel, and marinate overnight in 3 tbsp soy + 1 tbsp mirin + 3 tbsp water. The yolk should be jammy gold, not fully set.
  2. 02Make dashi: cold-steep kombu in 1 litre water for 30 minutes. Heat gently to 60°C — do not boil. Remove kombu. For non-vegetarian, add bonito flakes and steep off heat for 5 minutes, then strain. This is the base that everything else builds on.
  3. 03Add ginger to the dashi. Bring to a light simmer. Add grey oyster mushroom clusters and poach for 8 minutes. They will release umami into the broth and absorb the dashi in return.
  4. 04Remove from heat. Whisk miso paste into a small cup with 3 tbsp of the hot broth first, then stir back into the pot. Add soy sauce, mirin, and sesame oil. Do not boil after the miso goes in.
  5. 05Cook noodles separately. Drain. Place in warm bowls.
  6. 06Ladle hot broth and mushroom clusters over the noodles. Halve the marinated eggs and place cut-side up. Add nori, spring onion, sesame seeds. Finish with chilli oil. Eat immediately, in silence if possible.
Rahul's Note
Ramen is a ritual, not a quick lunch. Give it the time it needs. The grey oyster from Akaththi has a depth that I had only ever found in wild mushrooms in Japan — earthy, marine, slightly mineral. When it cooks in dashi, it tastes like it always belonged in a Japanese broth. I do not think that is an accident. I think it is the growing conditions here. The freshness matters enormously.
