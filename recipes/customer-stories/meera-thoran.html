Meera Krishnan — Adyar, Chennai
My mother has been making thoran for 40 years. The first time I substituted Akaththi pink oyster, she did not say a word. She just ate three helpings. That was enough.

Meera's version of kalan thoran is her mother's recipe, passed down as it is — unchanged except for the mushroom. The pink oyster turns it into something visually different but spiritually identical.

Ingredients
  • 250 g Pink Oyster mushrooms, torn by hand
  • 3/4 cup fresh coconut, grated
  • 2 green chillies, slit
  • 1/4 tsp turmeric
  • 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1/2 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • 2 dried red chillies, broken
  • Salt to taste
Meera's Method
  1. 01Coarsely grind coconut, green chillies, turmeric, and cumin — a few pulses only. Keep it rough. My mother always said a smooth coconut mixture is for chutney, not thoran.
  2. 02Heat coconut oil in a heavy pan — must be coconut oil, nothing else. Add mustard seeds and wait for every seed to pop. Add broken red chillies and curry leaves. Twenty seconds.
  3. 03Add mushrooms with a pinch of salt. Do not cover the pan. Stir-fry on medium-high 3–4 minutes until they release their water and it evaporates.
  4. 04Add coconut mixture. Stir well. Reduce heat to medium. Cover and cook 3 minutes. Uncover, stir once more, taste for salt. It should smell like the inside of a Tamil home.
  5. 05Serve alongside rice, sambar, and rasam. The pink oyster will have changed colour to a slightly deeper coral — it still looks beautiful against the white coconut.
Meera's Note
I order the pink oyster every week now. My mother asks me to bring extra. She says she prefers it to the button mushrooms she was using before, and she would never admit that about anything new. Pink oyster thoran has become her recipe now, not mine.
