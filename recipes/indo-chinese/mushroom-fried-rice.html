Golden Oyster / Grey Oyster

Oyster Mushroom Fried Rice

Wok-tossed Indo-Chinese

Time
20 min
Difficulty
Easy
Serves
2
Cuisine
Indo-Chinese

Day-old basmati, a ripping-hot wok, soy, spring onion, and golden oyster clusters that caramelise before the rice touches the pan. The mushrooms' subtle sweetness lifts this beyond standard fried rice.

Ingredients
  • 200 g Golden or Grey Oyster mushrooms, torn into pieces
  • 2 cups cooked basmati rice, day-old (cold from fridge)
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 1 tbsp garlic, minced
  • 1 egg (optional — skip for vegan)
  • 3 spring onions, sliced; whites and greens separated
  • 1/2 cup carrot, finely diced
  • 1/4 cup capsicum, diced
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp chilli sauce
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • Salt to taste
Method
  1. 01The rice must be cold and separated. Freshly cooked rice will clump. Use day-old rice from the fridge, or spread freshly cooked rice on a tray and refrigerate for at least an hour.
  2. 02Heat wok on high until very hot. Add oil, then garlic. Stir for 15 seconds. Add mushrooms — spread them out and let sit for 90 seconds before tossing. They should caramelise to golden at the edges, not steam.
  3. 03Push mushrooms to one side. If using egg, crack it into the cleared side and scramble until just set, then mix with the mushrooms.
  4. 04Add carrot, capsicum, and white spring onion. Toss for 2 minutes.
  5. 05Add the cold rice. Break up any clumps using the back of a spatula. Toss everything on high heat for 2–3 minutes until the rice is heated through and some grains are beginning to catch on the wok.
  6. 06Add soy sauce, chilli sauce, and pepper. Toss for 1 more minute. Finish with sesame oil and spring onion greens. Serve immediately.
Cook's Note
Golden oyster mushrooms caramelise faster than other varieties at high heat — their edges turn deeply golden and their mild, slightly sweet flavour balances the salty soy sauce perfectly. The caramelisation happens in the wok before the rice arrives; if you add rice too early, the mushrooms never get colour. Patience in that first step makes the dish.
