Grey Oyster / Elm Oyster

Doenjang Kalan Jjigae

Korean Fermented Soybean Stew

Time
30 min
Difficulty
Easy
Serves
2
Cuisine
Japanese / Korean

Korean fermented soybean paste, tofu, zucchini, and oyster mushrooms simmered in anchovy stock — earthy, funky, deeply restorative. The soup that Koreans have been eating for centuries and Chennai is just discovering.

Ingredients
  • 200 g Grey or Elm Oyster mushrooms, torn into large pieces
  • 200 g firm tofu, cubed
  • 1 small zucchini (courgette), sliced into half-moons
  • 1/2 medium onion, roughly chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp doenjang (Korean fermented soybean paste)
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes) — optional
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 600 ml anchovy stock or vegetable stock
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 green chilli sliced — optional
Method
  1. 01If making anchovy stock: simmer 6–8 dried anchovies and a piece of kombu in 700 ml water for 15 minutes. Strain. If using vegetable stock, ensure it has depth — a flat stock will make a flat jjigae.
  2. 02In a clay pot or heavy saucepan, heat sesame oil over medium. Add garlic and stir for 30 seconds. Add the doenjang and fry it in the oil for 1 minute, stirring — this blooms the paste and deepens its flavour.
  3. 03Add the onion and stir to coat with the paste. Pour in the stock. Bring to a boil.
  4. 04Add zucchini and mushrooms. Simmer for 8 minutes. Add tofu cubes gently — stir carefully to avoid breaking them.
  5. 05Add gochugaru if using. Simmer for another 3 minutes. Taste — the stew should be deeply savory, slightly fermented, and just warm on the heat. Do not add salt; doenjang is salty enough.
  6. 06Garnish with spring onion and sliced chilli. Serve in the pot it was cooked in, with steamed rice alongside. The rice goes in the bowl, not the stew — eat them together.
Cook's Note
Doenjang is available in Korean grocery stores and increasingly at major supermarkets in Chennai's Adyar and Nungambakkam neighbourhoods. It is not the same as Japanese miso — doenjang is chunkier, more fermented, and more pungent. The stew should taste funky and deep in the best possible way. Grey oyster mushroom's earthiness pairs directly with that fermented character.
