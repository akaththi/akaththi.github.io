Pink & Golden Oyster

Wild Forest Toast

Chef Priya — Akaththi Kitchen

Time
20 min
Difficulty
Medium
Serves
2
Cuisine
Chef Special

Multigrain sourdough, housemade curry-leaf labneh, roasted pink and golden oyster mushrooms, toasted black sesame, and a drizzle of cold-pressed coconut oil. Where Chennai meets the café table.

Chef Priya — Akaththi Kitchen
I wanted something that looked like it belonged on a brunch menu in London but tasted like home. The curry-leaf labneh was the bridge. Once I had that, the rest followed.
Ingredients
  • 100 g Pink Oyster mushrooms, fronds separated
  • 100 g Golden Oyster mushrooms, fronds separated
  • 2 thick slices multigrain or seeded sourdough
  • CURRY-LEAF LABNEH
  • 200 g thick Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1 sprig curry leaves (10–12 leaves)
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • Pinch of salt
  • TO FINISH
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp toasted black sesame seeds
  • 1/2 tsp chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp cold-pressed coconut oil
  • Flaky salt
  • Lemon 1/2 for squeezing
Method
  1. 01Make the curry-leaf labneh first — it can be done a day ahead. Heat coconut oil in a small pan until hot. Add curry leaves and fry 30 seconds until crisp. Remove, finely chop, and cool. Mix into Greek yoghurt with black pepper and salt. Refrigerate for at least 30 minutes. The labneh should be thick and spreadable.
  2. 02Toast the bread in a dry cast-iron pan until char lines appear and the surface is golden-brown and firm. Drizzle one side with olive oil while hot.
  3. 03In the same pan, heat butter and olive oil together over high heat until the butter foams. Add sliced garlic — 20 seconds. Add the pink and golden oyster fronds separately if possible; pink first as it cooks faster. Do not stir for 90 seconds. The frilled edges should crisp and the base turn golden.
  4. 04Toss gently, season with salt. Squeeze lemon juice over the mushrooms in the pan — it will spit. Remove from heat immediately.
  5. 05Spread curry-leaf labneh generously on the toast — at least 5 mm thick. Layer the mushrooms on top, mixing the pink and golden together. Scatter toasted black sesame seeds and chilli flakes. Finish with a slow drizzle of cold-pressed coconut oil and flaky salt.
  6. 06Serve immediately on a dark plate. The contrast of colours — pink, gold, white, black — is part of the dish.
Why it works
Curry leaf fried in coconut oil transfers its entire flavour into the fat. When you mix that fat into the yoghurt, you get a spread that is unmistakably South Indian but completely at home on a café plate. The black sesame is visual contrast and a subtle nutty note. Pink oyster at high heat crisps like no other mushroom — those frilled tips are the texture that makes the dish memorable.
