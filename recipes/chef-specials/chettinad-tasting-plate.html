Chef Arun — Akaththi Kitchen
Oyster mushrooms have taught me to slow down my masala. The spice must bloom in the fat, not drown in water. This plate puts all three varieties side by side so you can taste what we mean when we say each one is a different ingredient.

This tasting plate is three separate preparations served together — Varuval (dry fry), Kulambu (gravy), and Thoran (coconut stir-fry) — each using a different oyster mushroom variety. Serve with parotta, idiyappam, and steamed rice for the full experience.

Component 1 — Elm Oyster Varuval
  • 150 g Elm Oyster mushrooms, torn
  • 1 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper, coarsely cracked
  • 1/4 tsp fennel seeds
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • 2 cloves garlic, sliced
  • Salt to taste
  1. 01Heat oil until shimmering. Add fennel and curry leaves. Add garlic, then onion — cook until golden.
  2. 02Add mushrooms without water. Spread flat and leave 2 minutes. Toss, add pepper and salt. Stir-fry on high heat 4 minutes until dry and caramelised at the edges.
Component 2 — Grey Oyster Kulambu
  • 150 g Grey Oyster mushrooms, clusters intact
  • 1/2 cup Chettinad kulambu masala paste (see full Chettinad Kulambu recipe)
  • 1 medium tomato, chopped
  • 150 ml water
  • 1 tbsp coconut oil
  • Salt to taste
  1. 01Fry masala paste in oil for 3 minutes. Add tomato and cook until broken down.
  2. 02Add water and salt, bring to simmer. Add mushrooms, cook 10 minutes until gravy is thick and clings to the clusters.
Component 3 — Golden Oyster Thoran
  • 150 g Golden Oyster mushrooms, torn
  • 1/2 cup fresh coconut, grated
  • 1 green chilli, slit
  • 1/4 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1/2 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • Salt to taste
  1. 01Coarsely pulse coconut with green chilli and turmeric.
  2. 02Temper mustard seeds and curry leaves in oil. Add mushrooms with salt, stir-fry 3 minutes. Add coconut mixture, cook 3 minutes more.
Plating
  1. 01Use a wide, dark plate. Place a small mound of steamed rice at centre. Arrange each preparation around it — varuval dry and crumbly on one side, kulambu in a small pool beside it, thoran bright with coconut on the third side.
  2. 02Serve with a small parotta or idiyappam on the side and a wedge of lemon. Each preparation should be eaten in the same mouthful as the rice — the contrast between dry, gravy, and coconut is the entire point.
