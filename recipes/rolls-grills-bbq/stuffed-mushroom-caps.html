Ingredients
  • 12 large Elm Oyster mushroom caps, stems trimmed
  • FILLING
  • 4 tbsp butter
  • 1 medium onion, finely diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 cup mushroom stems + any small mushrooms, finely chopped
  • 1/2 cup breadcrumbs (panko preferred)
  • 3 tbsp cream cheese or thick labneh
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
  • 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves (or 1/2 tsp dried)
  • 3 tbsp Parmesan, grated (or any hard aged cheese)
  • Salt & black pepper generously
  • Olive oil for drizzling
Method
  1. 01Preheat oven to 200°C. Lightly brush the mushroom caps on both sides with olive oil. Place cup-side up on a baking tray. Season with salt and pepper.
  2. 02Make the filling: melt butter in a pan over medium heat. Add onion and cook until soft and translucent, about 5 minutes. Add garlic and chopped mushroom stems — cook for another 4 minutes until the moisture evaporates completely.
  3. 03Remove from heat. Stir in breadcrumbs, cream cheese, parsley, thyme, half the Parmesan, salt, and pepper. The filling should be moist enough to hold together but not wet — add a touch more breadcrumbs if it feels loose.
  4. 04Spoon filling generously into each mushroom cap, pressing it in and mounding it slightly above the rim. Sprinkle remaining Parmesan over the top of each.
  5. 05Bake for 18–20 minutes until the tops are golden and bubbling and the mushroom caps have softened and released some moisture at their base.
  6. 06Rest for 3 minutes before serving — the filling will be molten straight from the oven. Serve on a warm platter with toothpicks as an appetiser, or alongside a green salad as a starter.
Cook's Note
Elm oyster caps are structurally firm and cup-shaped, which makes them the best candidate for stuffing — they hold filling without collapsing under the heat of the oven. Use all the trimmings and any misshapen pieces in the filling itself; nothing is wasted. The cream cheese is what binds and enriches — labneh works equally well and adds a mild tanginess that cuts through the butter.
