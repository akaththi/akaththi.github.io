Grey Oyster / Elm Oyster

Kalan Milagu Varuval

Mushroom Pepper Fry

Time
20 min
Difficulty
Easy
Serves
2
Cuisine
South Indian

Black pepper, fennel, curry leaves, and torn oyster mushrooms roasted until the edges caramelise and the spices bloom dry. No water, no shortcuts. A weeknight dish that tastes like it took all afternoon.

Ingredients
  • 300 g Grey or Elm Oyster mushrooms, torn into large pieces
  • 2 tbsp coconut oil or sesame oil
  • 1 tsp black pepper, freshly cracked (generous)
  • 1/2 tsp fennel seeds (sombu)
  • 1/4 tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 sprig curry leaves (about 12 leaves)
  • 3 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1 small onion, sliced thin
  • 1 tsp salt, or to taste
  • 1/2 tsp red chilli powder (optional, for heat)
Method
  1. 01Heat oil in a heavy-bottomed pan or iron skillet on high heat until it begins to shimmer. Add fennel seeds and let them pop for 10 seconds.
  2. 02Add curry leaves — they'll spit. Add sliced garlic immediately and fry until the edges turn golden, about 45 seconds.
  3. 03Add sliced onion and cook on medium-high, stirring, until translucent and beginning to brown at the edges, about 4 minutes.
  4. 04Add turmeric and stir for 20 seconds, then add the torn mushroom pieces. Do not add water. Spread them out across the pan and leave undisturbed for 2 minutes so they begin to colour.
  5. 05Toss, then add freshly cracked black pepper, chilli powder if using, and salt. Stir-fry on high heat for another 3–4 minutes until the mushrooms are dry, caramelised at the edges, and the spices are clinging to every surface.
  6. 06Taste, adjust salt and pepper. Serve immediately with rice and rasam, or alongside idli and chutney.
Cook's Note
The temptation is to add water when the pan looks dry. Resist it. The mushroom holds its own moisture — the dry heat is what gives the varuval its character. If you are using grey oyster, tear the clusters rather than slicing them; the rough surface catches more spice.
