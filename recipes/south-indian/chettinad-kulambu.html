Elm Oyster / Grey Oyster

Chettinad Kalan Kulambu

Mushroom Chettinad Gravy

Time
40 min
Difficulty
Medium
Serves
4
Cuisine
South Indian

Stone-ground kalpasi, marathi mokku, and dried Guntur chillies build a thick brick-red kulambu that oyster mushrooms absorb like country chicken. Serve with parotta or idiyappam.

Ingredients
  • 350 g Elm or Grey Oyster mushrooms, kept in clusters
  • 2 medium onions, finely chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 3 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1/2 cup grated coconut, fresh
  • 6–8 dried red chillies (Guntur), stems removed
  • 1 tsp kalpasi (stone flower / dagad phool)
  • 1 marathi mokku (dried flower pod)
  • 1 tsp fennel seeds
  • 1 tsp coriander seeds
  • 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper, whole
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 1 inch ginger, peeled
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 1 sprig curry leaves
  • Salt to taste
Method
  1. 01In a dry pan on medium heat, toast the dried red chillies, kalpasi, marathi mokku, fennel, coriander, cumin, and black pepper for 2–3 minutes until aromatic. Transfer to a plate and cool.
  2. 02Dry-roast the grated coconut in the same pan, stirring continuously until it turns deep golden brown, about 5–6 minutes. This step is the heart of the kulambu.
  3. 03Grind the toasted spices, roasted coconut, garlic, and ginger together with a little water into a smooth, thick paste. Set aside.
  4. 04Heat oil in a heavy pot. Add curry leaves and onions. Fry on medium-high heat until the onions are deep golden brown, about 10 minutes — do not rush this step.
  5. 05Add tomatoes and turmeric. Cook until the tomatoes break down and the oil separates, about 7 minutes.
  6. 06Add the ground masala paste and fry for 4–5 minutes, stirring constantly to prevent sticking.
  7. 07Add 300 ml water and salt. Bring to a simmer. Add the mushroom clusters and cook on medium heat for 12–15 minutes, until the gravy thickens to your liking.
  8. 08Finish with a few drops of sesame oil if you like. Serve with parotta, idiyappam, or steamed rice.
Cook's Note
Kalpasi and marathi mokku are the two spices that make Chettinad cooking distinct from anything else. If you cannot source them, the kulambu will still be good — but not Chettinad. Look for them at any Sowcarpet spice merchant in Chennai. The dry roasting of coconut cannot be skipped; it gives the gravy its depth and colour.
