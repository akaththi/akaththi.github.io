Ingredients
  • 400 g Elm or Grey Oyster mushrooms, kept in whole clusters
  • 2 cups aged basmati rice, soaked 30 min
  • 3 large onions, thinly sliced
  • 3 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 1/2 cup curd (yoghurt), whisked
  • 4 tbsp ghee + 2 tbsp oil
  • A pinch saffron, soaked in 3 tbsp warm milk
  • 2 tbsp fresh mint leaves
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander leaves
  • Whole spices 2 bay leaves, 4 cloves, 3 cardamom, 1 inch cinnamon, 1 star anise
  • 1.5 tsp biryani masala powder
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • Salt to taste
Method
  1. 01Fry the sliced onions in 3 tbsp ghee + oil over medium-high heat, stirring often, until deeply golden and beginning to crisp — about 20 minutes. This is the barista (caramelised onion) that forms the biryani's backbone. Remove half and set aside for layering.
  2. 02In the same pan with remaining onions, add whole spices and stir for 30 seconds. Add tomatoes, turmeric, chilli powder, and biryani masala. Cook until the tomatoes break down and oil separates, about 8 minutes.
  3. 03Add the whisked curd gradually, stirring to prevent curdling. Cook for 3 minutes. Add mushroom clusters, stir to coat, and cook for 5 minutes. The mushrooms will release moisture — let it reduce slightly. Add salt.
  4. 04Cook the soaked rice in boiling salted water with 1 bay leaf until 70% cooked — still has a bite at the centre. Drain immediately.
  5. 05In a heavy-bottomed pot, layer: mushroom masala at the bottom, then partially cooked rice, then reserved fried onions, mint, coriander, and saffron milk drizzled over the top.
  6. 06Seal the pot tightly with a dough rope or heavy foil, then the lid. Cook on high heat for 3 minutes, then reduce to the lowest possible heat and cook dum for 20 minutes. Rest for 10 minutes before opening.
  7. 07Open gently, mixing from the bottom up. Serve with raita and pappad.
Cook's Note
The dum (sealed slow-cook) is what separates biryani from pulao. The steam circulates and finishes the rice while the mushrooms absorb the spiced vapour from below. Elm oyster mushrooms hold together in whole clusters through this process — do not tear or chop them. Grey oyster works equally well and gives a deeper, earthier flavour to the masala.
